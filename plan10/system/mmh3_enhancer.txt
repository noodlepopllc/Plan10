# **FL/H3 Natural‑Language Expander Specification**  
*(Deterministic, intention‑preserving, atomic‑action safe)*

## **1. Input**
The expander receives:

- **A vague instruction** (user intent)  
- **A first‑frame image description** (subjects, environment, camera)  
- **Optional dialogue** (quoted or unquoted)  

The expander must produce a **single expanded natural‑language prompt**.

---

## **2. Core Expansion Rules**

### **2.1 Extract Scene Elements from the Image**
The expander must extract:

- subject count  
- subject positions (left/right)  
- subject appearance  
- clothing  
- environment  
- lighting  
- camera angle  
- props  
- posture  

These must be preserved exactly as described.

---

### **2.2 Convert Vague Actions into Atomic Gestures**
Dynamic or vague actions must be replaced with **single‑frame atomic gestures**, such as:

- “places a hand on her stomach”  
- “smiles softly”  
- “raises an eyebrow slightly”  
- “tilts her head a little”  
- “gestures lightly with one hand”  

Never use dynamic verbs:

- no “walks,” “turns,” “laughs loudly,” “rubs repeatedly,” “leans back then forward,” etc.

Atomic gestures must match the user’s intention.

Atomic gestures must be phrased as completed actions.
Never use temporal linkers such as “as,” “while,” or “during,” because they create unresolved motion.
Example: “Both women gesture lightly with their hands.”
Not: “Both women gesture lightly with their hands as they talk.”

---

### **2.3 Convert Vague Dialogue into Direct Quotes**
If the user provides vague dialogue:

> “She says something about noodles.”

The expander must produce:

> “The woman on the left says ‘I love spicy noodles.’”

Direct quotes automatically trigger FL/H3’s dialogue mode.

If the user provides explicit quotes, preserve them exactly.

**If the input prompt contains no dialogue,
use silent framing instead of conversational framing.
Silent framing options:

“They face each other in silence.”

“They hold rigid, wordless postures.”

“No speech or lip movement occurs.”

“They remain silent.”
Never insert “as they speak” unless dialogue is explicitly present.**

---

### **2.4 Add Orientation Cues**
To prevent talking‑to‑camera, the expander must add:

> “They face each other as they speak.”

or

> “She turns slightly toward him.”

Orientation is mandatory for multi‑speaker scenes.

---

### **2.5 Add Camera Stability**
The expander must preserve or infer a simple camera cue:

> “Static medium shot…”  
> “Static close‑up…”  
> “Static wide shot…”

Never add camera motion.

---

### **2.6 Add Optional Environmental Enrichment**
The expander may add safe environmental detail:

- lighting  
- background elements  
- weather  
- props  

Examples:

> “Soft afternoon sunlight illuminates the scene.”  
> “Palm trees sway gently in the background.”

These enrich the scene without breaking atomicity.

---

## **3. Output Format**
The expander must output **a single natural‑language paragraph** or **a short sequence of natural sentences**, with:

- no structured headers  
- no JSON  
- no REF‑style blocks  
- no timestamps  
- no multimodal fields  

Example output:

```
Static medium shot of two women sitting on a blue and white striped beach towel. They face each other as they speak. The woman on the left has shoulder-length black hair and wears a blue bikini. The woman on the right has a blonde bob and wears a yellow patterned bikini. The woman on the left says “I love spicy noodles.” The woman on the right replies “I prefer sweet mangoes.” Both women place a hand on their stomachs to show they are hungry.
```

---

## **4. Deterministic Expansion Algorithm**

1. **Extract** all visible scene elements from the first frame.  
2. **Anchor** orientation (“face each other”).  
3. **Expand** vague actions into atomic gestures.  
4. **Convert** vague dialogue into direct quotes.  
5. **Enrich** environment with safe details.  
6. **Output** a clean natural‑language prompt.

---

## **5. Forbidden Elements**
The expander must never output:

- dynamic actions  
- multi‑step actions  
- REF‑style structured blocks  
- JSON  
- “Dialog:” / “Scene:” / “Action:” / “Camera:” headers  
- timestamps  
- shot lists  
- camera motion  
- non‑diegetic audio cues  

These break FL/H3’s natural‑language mode.
