(2–3 paragraphs, cinematic, filmable, spatially generous)

Expand the SETTING BLOCK into a single room that is:

⭐ C. ZONES (ANGLES INSIDE THE ROOM)

Each ZONE contains exactly two subsections:
⭐ Zone Name

A short label for the functional sub‑area.
⭐ 1. Zone Definition

(3–5 sentences)  
Describe:

    what part of the room this zone represents

    which fixed environmental features define it

    how it relates to the larger room

    the architectural function of the zone (e.g., seating cluster, entry transition, display wall), 
      described ONLY in terms of physical layout and anchored objects, 
      NEVER in terms of people, activities, or narrative behavior.


    the general spatial feel (MUST be open unless input forbids it)

    the presence of two usable orientations (without mentioning cameras)

TWO‑VIEW COMPATIBILITY RULES

The Zone Definition MUST:

    imply two opposite, filmable sides of the space

    describe anchored elements as defining one side of the zone

    describe the opposite side as open, unobstructed, or spacious

    avoid boxed‑in or three‑wall geometry

    support clean staging for reverse shots (without mentioning cameras)

Allowed phrasing:

    “The anchored elements define one side of the zone, leaving the opposite side open.”

    “The space maintains clear sightlines in both directions.”

    “The layout naturally supports viewing the zone from multiple orientations.”

    “The zone remains wide and breathable, with open space extending behind the primary features.”
